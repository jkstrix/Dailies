Create and Aeronautics

I

Scope

This note belongs with requirements MOD-1 to MOD-7 of the build specification. Those say what must hold. This says how it was made to hold in the owner's earlier private build, so the work does not have to be rediscovered.

Versions it was worked out on

  • Minecraft 1.21.1, NeoForge 21.1.266
  • Create 6.0.10
  • Sable 2.0.6 (the physics library; its packages start dev.ryanhcode.sable)
  • Create Aeronautics 1.3.2 (bundled build)

What kind of knowledge this is

  • Names of classes, methods and fields in Sable are given exactly. They were found by reading Sable and by experiment, and they hold for the version above only. Check each one before relying on it.
  • The techniques assume the playback design the specification recommends: the real client is connected to an internal stand-in that sends it the recorded data. If the core is built differently, the causes below still apply; the fixes will take a different shape.

Words

  • Stand-in: the internal thing the client is connected to during playback. It may hold its own copy of the world and entities.
  • Viewer: the client being shown the recording.
  • Sub-level: Sable's word for one physics object.
  • Plot: the hidden region of the world where Sable keeps a sub-level's blocks.

Rules that applied to every fix

Reflection onlyNothing compiles against Create or Sable. Every class is found by name and every method by name and argument types, once, then cached.
Off unless installedEach entry point first checks that the mod is loaded and returns at once when it is not.
Fail once, quietlyIf a lookup fails (a different Sable version), log one error that says what will look wrong in recordings, set a flag, and carry on without that feature. Never throw into the game.
Hooks that may be missingHooks into Sable's classes are declared so that a missing class or method is skipped, not an error.
II

Create contraptions

A contraption is the moving block structure carried by a mechanical bearing, piston, pulley or minecart. It is an entity, and its blocks are not in the world.

Seen
A bearing was recorded turning a structure. In playback the bearing was there and the structure was missing.
Cause
The contraption's blocks reach a client in NeoForge's extra spawn data: the payload AdvancedAddEntityPayload, sent with the entity's spawn for any entity that implements IEntityWithComplexSpawn. When the stand-in introduced the entity to the viewer, NeoForge built that payload afresh by asking the stand-in's copy of the entity to describe itself. That copy is an empty shell, created from the spawn packet alone, and Create's contraption entity threw a null pointer error while writing its data (in ControlledContraptionEntity.writeAdditional).
Fix
Never rebuild the payload. Keep the recorded one and send it again.

  1. While recording

    The payload arrives as an ordinary custom payload and is recorded like any other. Nothing special is needed for entities that spawn during the recording.

  2. In every snapshot

    For each known entity that implements IEntityWithComplexSpawn, build an AdvancedAddEntityPayload from it on the recording client and write it straight after that entity's spawn. Here the entity is real and can describe itself.

  3. During playback

    When such a payload comes up in the recorded stream, store it by its entity id before the entity is created. Whenever the stand-in introduces that entity to a viewer, send the stored payload in place of anything it would build itself.

  4. On a jump

    Empty the store when a snapshot is replayed; the snapshot refills it.

Movement and assembly state need no help: they arrive as entity data and as Create's own payloads, and replaying those is enough.

How Sable works

Everything in the next five chapters follows from four facts about Sable. Each caused its own fault.

IV

Recording motion

Seen
A platform was recorded drifting away. In playback it appeared, and never moved.
Cause
The recorder watches the normal connection. Sable's poses never cross it: they arrive over its UDP channel, in singleplayer as well.
Fix
Hook the point where Sable's client handles each movement packet, and record the packet there as if it had arrived as an ordinary custom payload.
Packet class (network.packets)HookWhat to do
ClientboundSableSnapshotDualPacket
the poses		Start of handleClient(Level)Record the packet object.
ClientboundSableSnapshotInfoDualPacket
the timing that goes with them		Start of handleClient(Level); also start and end of handleRecord the packet object, unless it is inside handle.
Why the second one needs careThe timing packet uses handleClient(Level) for both routes. When it does arrive over the normal connection, handle runs first and the recorder has already seen it. Set a per-thread flag at the start of handle, clear it at the end, and skip recording while it is set.
Recording itBoth packet classes are custom payloads. Wrap the object in a clientbound custom-payload packet and hand it to the recorder with the current tick, exactly as a received one would be.
Only while recording liveDo nothing when no recording is running, and nothing during playback, where the same handlers run on replayed data.
Playback needs nothingSable registers these same payloads for the normal connection too. Sent to the viewer as ordinary custom payloads, its client handles them unchanged.

This is the technique that has not been proven on a dedicated server, where the UDP channel is a real network path (MOD-7).

V

Showing the blocks

Seen
With motion recorded, the craft was still invisible in playback, though Sable reported that it existed.
Cause
The craft's blocks arrive as ordinary chunk and block-change packets for positions in the plot. The stand-in filed them into its own copy of the world, tens of millions of blocks from the viewer, and so never passed them on: nobody was near enough to be sent them.
Fix
Before the stand-in handles any recorded packet about a position, test whether that position is in the plot. If it is, send the packet straight to the viewer and do nothing else with it.

The test is SubLevelContainer.getContainer(level).inBounds(chunkPos). The packets it has to be applied to, and where each one's position comes from:

PacketPosition
Chunk with lightIts chunk x and z
Block update · block entity data · block event · block destruction · level eventIts block position
Section blocks updateIts section position (a private field in 1.21.1: needs an access change)
Light update · forget chunkIts chunk x and z
Particles · soundIts x, y, z
Every path to a chunkIf chunks stored once and shared between snapshots (FILE-5) take a different path through the stand-in than chunks in the stream, that path needs the same test. Missing it made the craft vanish after a jump only.
The stand-in's world may differThe test asks Sable about a world. Ask it about a world Sable knows on that side, and treat "no container" as "not in the plot".
VI

Entities aboard

Three faults, found one after another on a helicopter: the rotor vanished, then the seat stayed behind, then the pilot did.

Seen
The helicopter's body flew correctly. Its rotor blades, a Create contraption on a bearing, were missing.
Cause
The rotor is an entity whose position is in the plot. The stand-in created it in its own world there, where no chunks are loaded and no viewer is ever within range, so it was never sent to the viewer.
Fix
Keep a set of the entity ids that belong to craft. Send every packet about those entities straight to the viewer.

Sorting entity packets

The set starts empty and is emptied whenever a snapshot is replayed. Each recorded packet is sorted as follows.

PacketRuleThen
Add entityIf its position is in the plot, add its id to the set. If not, remove its id from the set (ids are reused).In the plot: send to the viewer only, then the marked teleport below. Otherwise handle normally.
Remove entitiesRemove each listed id from the set.If any was in the set: send to the viewer and also handle normally, since the stand-in may hold some of them too.
Set passengersLook at the vehicle's id.If it is in the set: send to the viewer and also handle normally.
Entity data · move · teleport · motion · head rotation · entity event · attributes · equipment · animate · leash link · the extra spawn data of chapter IILook at the entity's id.If it is in the set: send to the viewer only.
Anything elseHandle normally.

The entity id is a private field on the move, entity-event and head-rotation packets in 1.21.1. Those three need an access change.

Seen
The seat block flew with the helicopter, but the seat entity, and the pilot on it, hung in the air where the craft had been assembled.
Cause
Two things. Sable's client moves some kinds of entity out into the visible world when they spawn at plot coordinates, expecting the server to keep correcting them. And an entity that existed before assembly (the seat a player sat on first) is moved into the plot afterwards by a position update, not by a spawn, so the set never learned of it.
Fix
Send marked teleports, and watch position updates for entities crossing into the plot.

  1. The marked teleport

    Build an ordinary teleport-entity packet for the entity's id, position and rotation (rotations as bytes: degrees × 256 ÷ 360, rounded down). Then call the method sable$setActuallyInSubLevel(true) on the packet object. Sable adds that method to the teleport packet class; its own server sets the same mark for entities that are part of a craft. A marked teleport to plot coordinates keeps the entity in the plot.

  2. After a spawn in the plot

    Having sent the add-entity packet to the viewer, send a marked teleport to the same position and rotation straight after it.

  3. On every recorded position update

    For an entity the stand-in knows that is not a passenger: if the new x and z are in the plot, add its id to the set, send the viewer a marked teleport, and keep it out of the stand-in's ordinary position broadcasting. If they are not, remove its id from the set and treat it as usual. For an entity the stand-in does not know, send a marked teleport when the position is in the plot and a plain one when it is not.

  4. Stop the stand-in removing it

    Once the stand-in's copy of an entity sits in the plot, its tracking sees it as impossibly far from every viewer and tells them to remove it. Suppress that removal for entities whose position is in the plot. Real removals are in the recording and are passed on by the table above.

ResultRotor blades spin, the seat stays on the craft, and a seated player moves and turns with it, including when they sat down before the craft was assembled.
Not solved: standing passengersA player standing loose on a deck is carried by Sable's client-side logic in live play, not by packets. In playback their recorded world positions are a fraction of a tick behind the craft, and they trail about a block behind a fast climb. This is requirement MOD-4. The likely route is to express a standing passenger's recorded position relative to the craft, using the pose at the same tick, and place them from the craft's interpolated pose each frame.
VII

Craft in snapshots

Seen
A craft assembled in the first few minutes was missing after jumping to any later segment.
Cause
A snapshot describes the world a fresh client would need, built from the vanilla world. Sable's sub-levels are not in it.
Fix
Add each sub-level to every snapshot (REC-23), using the same three messages a Sable server sends when a player comes into range of one.

For each sub-level from getAllSubLevels() whose isRemoved() is false, write in this order:

  1. Start tracking

    Payload class network.packets.tcp.ClientboundStartTrackingSubLevelPacket, a record. Its arguments in order: the plot coordinate; getUniqueId(); the pose (logicalPose()); the pose again, as the previous pose; the plot's getBoundingBox(); getName(); the game tick. Build it through the record's canonical constructor.

  2. The plot's chunks

    For every entry of the plot's getLoadedChunks(), take getChunk() and write an ordinary chunk-with-light packet for it, using the plot's own getLightEngine(), not the world's.

  3. Finalize

    If isFinalized(), payload class network.packets.tcp.ClientboundFinalizeSubLevelPacket, a record of one argument: the plot coordinate.

ValueWhere it comes from
Plot coordinateThe plot's plotPos minus the container's origin (getOrigin(), fields x and y), packed as a chunk position long: ChunkPos.asLong(plotPos.x − origin.x, plotPos.z − origin.y).
Game tickThe container's getInterpolation() has a private numeric field mostRecentTick; use it rounded down, never below 0.
All or nothing per craftCollect a craft's packets in a list and write them only once all three steps have succeeded, so a failure half way never leaves a craft that is tracked but has no blocks.
Entities aboardThey are ordinary entities at plot positions and are written by the normal entity part of the snapshot. Chapter VI's sorting picks them up when the snapshot is replayed.
Block entities that cannot answerBuilding a chunk packet on a client asks each block entity for its update data. See chapter IX for the one that throws.
VIII

Jumps

Seen
After jumping backwards, the log filled with "plot already exists" errors and craft were duplicated or stuck.
Cause
A backward jump replays a snapshot, which creates every craft again. Sable refuses to create one that already exists.
Fix
Before a snapshot is replayed to the viewer, remove every sub-level from the viewer's world: for each of getAllSubLevels() (copy the collection first), call removeSubLevel(subLevel, SubLevelRemovalReason.REMOVED) on the container. Do it at the same moment the viewer's ordinary entities are cleared.
Seen
After a jump, a craft that had moved during the skipped stretch sat where it had been at the start of that stretch, then snapped to its true place seconds later, or never.
Cause
A jump delivers minutes of recorded poses at once. Sable's smoother (network.client.SubLevelSnapshotInterpolator) discards every pose older than its current time minus 6 ticks before it samples. All the poses of the skipped stretch are older than that, so they were thrown away unused.
Fix
Hook the start of the smoother's tick(double backTick). During playback only: look at its private list field buffer; if the newest entry's gameTick() is below backTick − 6 (so the whole buffer is about to be dropped), copy that newest entry's pose() into the smoother's private field runningSnapshot by calling its one-argument set(pose). The craft then holds its last known pose.
Lock as Sable doesRead the buffer while synchronised on it; it is filled from another thread.
Harmless noiseDuring a jump Sable logs errors about a "movement packet for a non-existent sub-level" when recorded motion arrives for a craft that had not been created yet at that point of the stream. Nothing is wrong and nothing needs fixing.
IX

Other faults on the way

Seen
With a hot air balloon nearby and its burner lit, the game crashed at the end of every recording segment.
Cause
A snapshot builds chunk packets from the client's chunks, which asks each block entity for its update data. Vanilla only ever asks on a server. Aeronautics' burner (aeronautics:adjustable_burner) assumes so and fails with a class-cast error on a client, where its balloon is the client-side kind.
Fix
This is REC-22. Where a chunk packet gathers a block entity's update data on a client, catch any failure. Fall back to the block entity's plain save data without metadata; if that fails too, use an empty tag. Warn once per block entity type, saying its state may reset after a jump to a later part. Make the surrounding snapshot work null-safe as well, so one bad chunk is skipped.
Seen
Playing a recording back, Sable tried to open a network channel, froze the viewer, or put them into an editing mode.
Cause
Three of Sable's payloads act on the recorder's own connection or body, and were being replayed.
Fix
Put sable:udp_activation, sable:freeze_player and sable:enter_gizmo_mode on the default playback ignore list (MOD-6).
Seen
Opening a recording failed or changed the world's biomes with certain mods installed.
Cause
NeoForge runs its server-start steps for any integrated server, among them applying biome modifiers to the world's registries. A playback world is not a world to modify.
Fix
This is PLAY-6. Skip those world-altering start-up steps when the server starting is the playback stand-in.
Seen
Any modded entity with extra spawn data, not only contraptions, could appear wrong or crash on being introduced to the viewer.
Fix
Chapter II covers all of them: it is keyed on NeoForge's interface, not on Create.
X

What the Smart Camera needs

The provided Smart Camera asks eight questions about craft through Host.Crafts. This is how each was answered. All of it sits behind one lazy set-up that looks the names up once and switches the whole feature off, with one log line, if any is missing.

QuestionAnswer
isInPlot(level, x, z)SubLevelContainer.getContainer(level).inBounds(chunk of x, z).
projectOutOfSubLevel(level, position)Sable.HELPER.projectOutOfSubLevel(level, position). Returns the position unchanged when it is not in a plot, or when the result is null.
projectOutOfSubLevel(level, position, partialTick)Find the craft with HELPER.getContaining(level, position); take its pose as drawn this frame; return pose.transformPosition(position).
projectDirectionOutOfSubLevelThe same, with pose.transformNormal(direction).
getSubLevel(entity)The first that answers: HELPER.getContaining(entity) (the entity is part of a craft), then HELPER.getTrackingOrVehicleSubLevel(entity) (it rides one), then the search below (it stands on one).
getSubLevelAt(level, position)HELPER.getContaining(level, position).
getSubLevelBounds(subLevel)boundingBox(), read through minX() to maxZ(), as a world-space box. Null if any value is not finite or the box is inside out.
isNearSubLevel(subLevel, position, margin)Whether those bounds, grown by the margin, contain the position.

The pose as drawn this frame

If the object is a ClientSubLevel and renderPose(float) exists, use renderPose(partialTick). Otherwise fall back to logicalPose(), which is a tick old. The fallback makes the camera judder on a moving craft, so it matters that the first works.

Finding what an entity stands on

Sable only knows what an entity stands on for entities this client simulates, and recorded players are not simulated. So: for each craft whose bounds, grown by 1, contain the entity's feet, take the feet position at 0.1, 0.6 and 1.1 blocks down, turn each into plot coordinates with logicalPose().transformPositionInverse, and look at the block there. If any has a collision shape, the entity is standing on that craft.

Create contraptions

For framing a vanilla vehicle or a contraption the camera needs no Sable: it uses the entity's bounding box. A contraption is recognised by its entity type id being in the create namespace and containing "contraption".

XI

Proven and open

Proven by the scripted run

  • A bearing contraption appears and turns, live and in playback.
  • A physics platform keeps its recorded position.
  • A hot air balloon climbed 82 blocks with a player aboard. At three points of the climb, each reached by a jump, playback had the balloon within 0.3 to 0.5 of a block of its live position.
  • Craft survive jumps forward across segments and jumps backward, with no duplicates.
  • The lit burner no longer breaks segment snapshots.

Proven by eye, flown by hand

  • A helicopter: body, spinning rotor and seated pilot all move together in playback.
  • Sitting down before assembling the craft works.

Open

  • Standing passengers trail the craft (chapter VI). In the balloon test the passenger was about 1.4 blocks low.
  • A dedicated server, where UDP is a real path. Never tried.
  • Other players riding a craft. Never tried.
  • Sitting down after the craft is assembled. Never tried.
  • Glue entities may show a stale position during forward play.
  • The burner's state after a jump, since its snapshot data is the fallback.
  • Other Aeronautics parts: levitite, propellers on planes, anything not in the two test craft.
